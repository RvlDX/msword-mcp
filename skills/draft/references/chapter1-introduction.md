# Chapter I Introduction

Follow the six headings of the outline. Keep the background an introduction to the whole document. Save the detail for the later chapters.

## 1.1 Background of the Problem

Build a funnel in this order.

1. Host or field context relevant to the work.
2. The actual operational or technical process currently used.
3. Observable factual symptoms, with source and evidence (who observed, when, where, frequency or impact if measurable).
4. Why the problem matters for the host, the user, or the process.
5. At least one relevant previous journal citation. The cited journal must show its journal name and ISSN or volume number.
6. The gap, or the reason for analysis and recommendation in this context.
7. The scope transition to the study focus.

Never use generic digital-transformation claims as a substitute for company evidence. Distinguish symptoms from root causes. Cite facts from the host appropriately and protect confidential information.

## 1.2 Problem Identification

Turn the central factual problems reflected in the background into concise declarative statements, not questions. Each item must be answerable through the analysis. Map each statement to supporting evidence.

## 1.3 Scope and Limitations

Define the process, system, or module, the user or unit, the location, the period, the data scope, the technology boundary, and the exclusions. Boundaries govern what Chapter IV and Chapter V cover. State limitations that leave sensible room for later work. Never claim broader coverage than the evidence permits.

## 1.4 Problem Formulation

Convert the bounded core issue into clear research questions. Ensure each question maps to an identified problem and can be answered in the report. Avoid solution-leading questions that assume the proposed technology is already selected.

## 1.5 Objectives and Benefits

Write specific objectives that are measurable in principle, using verbs such as analyze, identify, design, or evaluate (only where an evaluation is actually conducted). Separate objectives from practical benefits to the host, users, student, or discipline. Note that some D3 outlines use the wording Objectives and Benefits of Research while S1 outlines use Objectives and Benefits of Writing. Follow the outline of the student program.

## 1.6 Writing Systematics

Describe the actual contents of each chapter briefly, consistent with the submitted report. Never write a generic template that does not match the actual outline.
