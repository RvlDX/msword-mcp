# Chapter IV Results and Discussion

## Current state first

Describe the running system or the field situation as it works now, with real data and real flow. Never present the proposed system as the current system.

- Company or field overview: history, organization structure with its functions, and the context the reader needs.
- System description: the process in full and in sequence, with actors or devices, inputs, activities, outputs, data stores, external interfaces, control points, and handoffs where relevant.
- Diagrams: select a diagram because it clarifies the real case, and confirm with the supervisor as the guide directs. For document and data procedures use a flowmap or flowchart. For networks use topology or architecture. For IoT use IoT architecture. Keep symbols, labels, and narrative consistent.

## Problem analysis

Start by understanding the problem domain, analyzing problems and opportunities and the business process, and defining improvement objectives. Requirement discovery identifies and understands what the system should do and what properties and quality it should have. Choose a documented method appropriate to the evidence and the problem. Never mechanically fill every category without evidence. A category can be marked no evidence found yet or out of scope.

- PIECES: Performance, Information, Economics, Control, Efficiency, Service. Ask the guiding question of each dimension and answer only from evidence.
- SWOT: Strengths and Weaknesses are internal, Opportunities and Threats are external. Build SO, ST, WO, and WT strategies only from evidenced factors.
- Information Characteristics: relevance, accuracy, timeliness, economics, efficiency, reliability, and usefulness.

## Proposed system

Present a global design aligned with the analysis and the study program, not an unjustified implementation commitment. Include architecture or workflow, scope, users, inputs, processes, outputs, data stores, controls and security, and the diagrams relevant to the program. Match the diagrams to the topic.

- Business and information systems: flowchart or flowmap for procedures, UML use case, activity diagram, and wireframe for computerized processes.
- Networks: proposed topology or network architecture blueprint.
- IoT: proposed block diagram, flowchart, full IoT blueprint, needed hardware and software, and preparation for applying it.
- AI: proposed AI algorithm, flowchart, and AI-based application design. State input and features, model and inference stage, output, users, validation assumptions, and limits. Never claim accuracy without experiments.
- Mobile: flowchart or flowmap, UML use case, activity diagram, and wireframe.
- Game: the mobile set plus a storyboard.
- Web and desktop: flowchart or flowmap, plus UML use case, activity diagram, and wireframe for computerized processes.

Discuss feasibility, dependencies, risks, and migration or training considerations where material. Explicitly link each proposed feature to one or more evidenced findings.

## Results and discussion

Report the implementation results and show that the built system answers the problems. Present results before interpretation, compare with the related research from Chapter II, and explain where the results agree or differ and why. Never claim an unmeasured improvement.
