# Chapter II Literature Review

The standard outline shows only two top-level sections: 2.1 Literature Review and 2.2 Related Research. More granular subsections may be used when appropriate, but confirm with the supervisor if outline restrictions are uncertain.

## 2.1 Literature Review

Use current primary literature relevant to the topic. Primary sources are articles, journals, monographs, and original writings. Explain concepts that are used in the later analysis or design. Never pad with unrelated definitions. Connect each theory to where it is used in Chapter IV or Chapter V.

## 2.2 Related Research

Use related research from ISSN-bearing journals. Compare rather than list. For each study record author and year, problem and context, method, technology or model, result, limitation, and relevance and difference to this research. Never invent paper metadata, DOI, ISSN, volume, pages, findings, or access dates.

## Reference age and count

Guides differ on reference age and count, and the difference must be flagged, not silently resolved.

- Some guides ask for literature from the 5 years before the research starts, for the literature review and related research.
- Some guides ask for references within the last 10 years for the bibliography.
- Some guides ask for a fixed count at the defense, for example 10 journal papers and 8 books, with a stated distribution across chapters.

Treat these as separate guide statements. When the guide the user follows is unclear on a category, flag it for supervisor confirmation and mark `[CHECK: ...]`. Never quietly reinterpret a count or a window.
