# Keep it: conventions that must not be corrected

An over-eager editor can damage academic prose that is actually correct. The items below are legitimate conventions. Never change them unless the target journal style asks for it.

## Passive di- in Methods

Indonesian is comfortable with passive voice, and the Method chapter holds procedures whose actor does not matter. "Sampel diambil dengan teknik purposive sampling" is already good. Fix only stacked passives or passives plus nominalization ("telah dilakukan pengambilan sampel"). Never force every sentence into active voice the way English editing guides suggest.

## "Penelitian ini" and the impersonal point of view

"Penelitian ini bertujuan ..." and "Hasil penelitian menunjukkan ..." are standard patterns in Indonesian prose. Never replace them with "kami" when the text is consistently impersonal. Conversely, never replace "kami" with impersonal forms when the target journal uses "kami".

## Foreign terms standard in the field

Terms such as machine learning, focus group discussion, stunting, grounded theory, literacy, or self-efficacy are often kept in their original form because the reader of that field does not know the Indonesian equivalent. Make sure the term is italicized and consistent. Never force an equivalent such as "mangkus" for efficient or "pemelajaran mesin" when the target journal uses machine learning.

## Legitimate hedging

"Diduga", "cenderung", "kemungkinan", "mengindikasikan", and "sejalan dengan" show a claim strength that matches the evidence. Never delete them to make a sentence sound firm. Fix only stacked hedging ("diduga kemungkinan dapat").

## "Signifikan" in the statistical sense

"Terdapat perbedaan yang signifikan (p = 0,03)" is correct. The word is only a problem when used without a statistical test or strengthened into "sangat signifikan".

## Long sentences in the humanities

History, philosophy, literature, and religious studies often use longer sentences and longer paragraphs than engineering texts. That is a field convention, not an error, as long as the main sentence stays clear.

## Quotes, titles, and names

Never edit a direct quote, a work title, an institution name, a personal name, an interview quote, or a transcript. Keep old spelling inside historical source quotes (for example "Djakarta" in a 1950s archive). When the quoted source has an error, add [sic] and never fix it.

## Examples under discussion

In linguistics or language education texts, a wrong sentence is often quoted as data ("Siswa menulis dimana serangkai"). Never "fix" the data.

## Author voice

When the user gives a sample of their own writing, follow their sentence length, word choice, and paragraph habits. The patterns in references/ai-patterns.md are acted on only when they really disturb. A consistent author voice is worth more than sterile prose.

## Forms that are correct but often suspected

| Form | Why it is correct |
|---|---|
| bukan hanya ..., tetapi juga ... | standard correlative conjunction, only repeated use is a problem |
| di mana (question word) | "Di mana sampel disimpan?" |
| lebih dari 50 orang | "lebih dari" for numbers, not comparison |
| dipengaruhi | the di- prefix does not melt the "p" |
| mengkritik, memproduksi | consonant clusters do not melt |
| terdiri dari | not wrong by rule, "terdiri atas" is only a recommendation |
| Oleh karena itu | standard, only a problem when repeated in every paragraph |
