# Genre: Research (skripsi, proposal, tesis, disertasi, journal article)

Use this template for research documents. Fill the 14 variables in guide-adapter.md first. When the guide names a chapter differently, use the guide name and keep the job of the section the same.

## Outline template

For each section write the heading, the Job, 2 to 5 content points, the Evidence, and the Length hint.

### Chapter I Introduction

- 1.1 Background of the Problem. Job: make the reader believe the problem is real, specific, and worth solving. Content: host or field context, the current process, observable symptoms with source, why it matters, at least one prior journal citation, the gap. Evidence: field facts, documents, or `[NEED: ...]`.
- 1.2 Problem Identification. Job: list the central factual problems as declarative statements, each answerable through the analysis. Content: one statement per problem, each mapped to supporting evidence.
- 1.3 Scope and Limitations. Job: state what is in scope and what is out, so Chapter IV and Chapter V stay bounded. Content: process, system or module, user or unit, location, period, data scope, technology boundary, exclusions.
- 1.4 Problem Formulation. Job: turn the bounded core issue into clear research questions. Content: questions that map to identified problems and can be answered in the report. Avoid solution-leading questions that assume the proposed technology is already selected.
- 1.5 Objectives and Benefits. Job: state specific objectives and separate them from benefits. Content: objectives with verbs such as analyze, identify, design, or evaluate (only where an evaluation is actually conducted). Benefits for the host, users, student, or discipline.
- 1.6 Writing Systematics. Job: describe the actual contents of each chapter briefly, consistent with the submitted report. Never write a generic template that does not match the actual outline.

### Chapter II Literature Review

- 2.1 Literature Review. Job: present the theories and concepts the analysis actually uses. Content: current primary literature, each concept connected to where it is used in Chapter IV or Chapter V. Never pad with unrelated definitions.
- 2.2 Related Research. Job: compare, not list. Content per study: author and year, problem and context, method, technology or model, result, limitation, relevance and difference to this research. Never invent paper metadata, DOI, ISSN, volume, pages, or findings.

### Chapter III Research Methods

- 3.1 Data Collection Techniques. Job: state how data is collected. Content: observation, interview, literature study, questionnaire, or experiment, with who, when, and how many.
- 3.2 Development Model or Research Design. Job: name the design and justify it. Content: quantitative, qualitative, mixed, or R and D, with the development model when the work builds a system.
- 3.3 Thinking Framework. Job: draw how the problem, the method, and the proposed system connect. Content: a diagram plus one paragraph explaining it.

### Chapter IV Results and Discussion

- Company or field overview. Job: give the context the reader needs. Content: history, organization, and the running system as it works now, with real data and real flow.
- Problems found. Job: list the problems in the running system, backed by observed data.
- Alternative solutions. Job: present options and pick one with reasons.
- Proposed system design. Job: present the design with diagrams, data design, and interface design.
- Implementation results. Job: show that the built system answers the problems, with test results.

### Chapter V Conclusions and Suggestions

- 5.1 Conclusions. Job: answer the formulation questions from the results and the discussion. Content: short conclusions, each tied to the earlier discussion and its implication. Never introduce new facts.
- 5.2 Suggestions. Job: give actionable recommendations. Content: suggestions for the host, for the system, and for further research, prioritized by value and feasibility.

## Traceability mapping (Full mode)

| Question | Objective | Chapter | Evidence | Conclusion | Suggestion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [question] | [objective] | [section] | [data or source] | [answer] | [recommendation] |

Every row must be complete. An empty cell is an orphan. Report it as a finding.

## Title pattern

Build titles from the core claim. Keep the campus title rules from the filled variables, for example a word limit. Never promise in the title what the work does not deliver.
