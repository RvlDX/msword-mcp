# Layered quality control

Stage gates check inputs/readiness; layers check document quality. Apply only relevant layers.

## Layer 1: style and readability
Remove empty openers, filler, repeated connectors, inflated claims, and machine-like patterns only when they weaken the text. Preserve author voice, field conventions, hedging, facts, quotations, citations, and claim strength. Mark prose checks Not applicable for non-prose artifacts.

## Layer 2: mechanics and language
Apply the required language standard (EYD V/KBBI for Indonesian). Check spelling, grammar, punctuation, units, abbreviations, spacing, capitalization, and typography against the guide. Preserve technical terms, variables, formulas, names, and quotations. Similarity tools are signals, not verdicts.

## Layer 3: semantic and evidentiary consistency
Check terminology, factual support, inference labels, numerical consistency, method/data fit, conclusions, and recommendation traceability. Research alignment checks apply only to research. Current/proposed-state checks apply to relevant technical and professional reports.

## Layer 4: red-team review
Read as a skeptical but fair evaluator appropriate to the document. Ask: Is purpose clear? Is the central claim/result/recommendation supported? What is the most consequential weakness? Is the approach feasible with available evidence, data, time, and resources? Does it meet requirements that actually apply? Is any statement stronger than its evidence? What prevents the next review/submission stage? Can the author explain key decisions?

For proposals, assess gap, method, and scope. For articles, assess venue fit, contribution, method/result integrity, and reporting. For placement/technical reports, assess evidence for process and recommendation.

## Pass rubric
Do not invent a weighted score or pass threshold. Use criterion-level findings unless a user-supplied rubric specifies weights. A layer not relevant to the artifact is Not applicable.
