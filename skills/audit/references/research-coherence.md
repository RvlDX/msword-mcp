# Research Coherence Audit

## Purpose

This audit tests whether the research forms a defensible chain of reasoning, not merely whether it follows a campus template. Separate **compliance** (rule adherence) from **coherence** (continuity of logic) and **validity** (method fit and evidence support). A document that follows the format may still have academic problems.

## Research chain

Trace the chain actually used: **context/problem → problem evidence → gap/rationale → research question → objective/hypothesis → theory/concepts → design/method → population/materials/data → instrument/procedure → analysis → results → discussion → conclusion → contribution/recommendation**. For each link, ask whether the next part answers, operationalizes, or depends on the previous one.

Do not require the number of questions, objectives, and conclusions to always match. One objective may answer several questions if the mapping is clear.

## Dimensions

| ID | Dimension | Core check |
|---|---|---|
| RC01 | Problem and gap | Is the problem evidenced and does the gap follow from the background/literature? |
| RC02 | Questions, objectives, hypotheses, conclusions | Are questions answered by objectives, method, and conclusions? |
| RC03 | Literature and theory | Is theory/concepts used, not merely presented? |
| RC04 | Constructs and operationalization | Are variables, definitions, indicators, instruments consistent? |
| RC05 | Method fit | Can the design and method answer the questions? |
| RC06 | Data and analysis | Do sample/materials, instruments, collection, and analysis fit each other? |
| RC07 | Results and discussion | Does interpretation match the results actually obtained? |
| RC08 | Claims and evidence | Do claims, contributions, and conclusions stay within the evidence? |
| RC09 | Cross-section consistency | Are terms, numbers, samples, locations, periods, methods, tables, and figures consistent? |
| RC10 | Overall logic | Are there logical leaps, circular reasoning, unfulfilled promises, or scope drift? |

## Adaptation by research design

- **Quantitative/survey:** theory → constructs → hypotheses/questions → indicators → instrument → sample → data quality/assumptions → analysis → interpretation. Distinguish association from causality and significance from effect size.
- **Qualitative/case study:** phenomenon → questions → context/informants → collection → corpus → coding/interpretation → themes with traceable quotes → bounded conclusions. Do not force statistical validity terms.
- **Mixed methods:** audit each strand, then check the rationale for combining, the integration point, and the combined inference. Two methods in one document are not necessarily integrated mixed methods.
- **System development:** actual problem → needs → requirements → design → implementation status → test per requirement → test results → acceptance/use claims. Distinguish analysis, proposal, prototype, implementation, deployment, and evaluation.
- **ML/data mining:** question → dataset/target → unit of analysis → preprocessing → split → leakage prevention → baseline → metrics → results → error analysis → generalization limits.
- **SLR/review:** question → protocol/search → databases/dates → criteria → screening → corpus → extraction → synthesis. Check count reconciliation and reproducibility.

## Issue types

- **Contradiction:** two statements cannot both be true under the same scope/definitions.
- **Misalignment:** a part does not answer or operationalize the promise of another part.
- **Unsupported inference:** a conclusion is stronger than the results/evidence.
- **Missing bridge:** a leap from premise to conclusion without the reasoning needed.
- **Terminology drift:** the meaning of a term/variable changes silently.
- **Scope drift:** population, period, location, unit, or outcome changes across sections.
- **Not assessable:** the data, instrument, appendix, or source needed is unavailable.
- **Acceptable variation:** the difference is genuinely justified by the research design.

Every finding must include location, exact quote, related sections, academic reasoning, impact, an actionable recommendation, and additional evidence where needed. Fix the most upstream root cause before its derivatives.
